Coast

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 August 2026

The short version

Coast records your rides on your phone. There is no Coast account and no server of ours — your rides are never sent to us.

Your GPS track and motion sensor readings are written to a database and to CSV files in the app's own private storage on your device. The app never uploads them. There is no login, and nothing of ours holds a copy. If you want a ride log somewhere else, you export it yourself with the share sheet.

One caveat we would rather state plainly than bury: on iPhone, Coast's rides sit in the app's Documents folder, which Apple includes in your device backup. If you have iCloud Backup switched on, a copy of your rides is in that backup. It is your backup, it goes to your Apple account, and we never receive it and cannot read it. On Android, Coast switches backup off entirely, so nothing is copied off the phone. Both cases are explained below.

The rest of this page explains all of that in detail, and is honest about the few things that do leave your phone — map tiles, purchases, and push notifications.

What Coast collects, and when

Location

While a ride is running, Coast records your GPS location to draw your route and calculate distance, speed, and other ride statistics.

  • Recording only happens during a ride you explicitly started. It begins when you start a ride and stops when you stop it.
  • Outside a ride, Coast takes a location reading when the app starts and when you open the Ride screen, purely to centre the map where you are — first whatever position your phone already has cached, then a fresh fix. Those readings are not recorded or stored, and they are only taken if you have already granted location permission. Opening the app never triggers a permission prompt on its own.
  • Coast is foreground only. It never asks for background or “Always” location: on Android it does not declare the background location permission, and on iPhone it asks only for “While Using the App”. If Coast leaves the foreground, the ride stops recording and tells you that it did.
  • Location data is written straight to local storage on your device.

Motion sensors

During a ride, Coast also reads your phone's motion sensors (accelerometer and gyroscope) to understand how the ride is going — acceleration, braking, cornering, and similar. Same rules as location: only during a ride, only stored on the device.

What Coast does not collect

  • No name, no email address, no phone number.
  • No account, no password, no sign-in of any kind.
  • No contacts, photos, microphone, or camera access.
  • No advertising SDKs.
  • No tracking of you across other apps or websites.

Where your ride data lives

All ride data is stored only on your device, in two places inside the app's own private storage:

  • A local SQLite database of your rides and their statistics.
  • CSV files, one per ride, holding the recorded data points.

That storage is sandboxed — other apps on your phone cannot read it — and Coast never uploads it. There is no server of ours holding a copy.

Backups: this part genuinely differs by phone

On Android, Coast switches automatic backup off. Many apps let Android copy their data to your Google Drive in the background; Coast opts out of both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer, so your rides are not silently duplicated off the phone. The trade-off is deliberate: if you move to a new phone or reinstall the app, your ride history does not come with you. Export anything you want to keep first.

On iPhone, we cannot honestly make that claim, so we will not. Coast's ride database and CSV logs live in the app's own Documents folder, and Apple includes that folder in your device backup. With iCloud Backup switched on — which is the default — a copy of your rides forms part of that backup, the same as most apps' data. The same applies to a backup you make to your own computer.

That backup is yours, not ours. It goes to your Apple account under Apple's privacy policy rather than this one; we never receive it, and we could not read it if we wanted to. If you would rather your rides were not included in your iCloud backup, switch Coast off under Settings → [your name] → iCloud → Manage Account Storage → Backups, or turn iCloud Backup off.

Exporting your rides

Settings contains a developer section — revealed by tapping the version row seven times — with an export option that hands your raw ride CSVs to your phone's system share sheet, so you can send them to yourself or to another app.

This is entirely user-initiated. Nothing is shared unless you tap export and then choose a destination. Once you share a file with another app or service, that data is in their hands and this policy no longer covers it — their privacy policy does.

Services Coast connects to

Coast is not fully offline. Here is every third party the app talks to and exactly what they get.

The lists below name everything Coast itself chooses to send. Two of these services — RevenueCat and OneSignal — are software kits that run inside the app, and like almost every such kit they attach ordinary technical metadata of their own to the requests they make: typically your device model, its operating system version, the app and kit version, your language and your time zone. Every internet request also carries your device's IP address, from which a service can work out roughly what country you are in. We do not choose to send any of that, and we cannot switch it off; it comes with using their service at all. We require each of these companies to protect what they receive to at least the standard described in this policy, and to use it only to provide their service to Coast. None of them receives your routes, your ride history, or your raw sensor data. Their own privacy policies are linked in each section.

RevenueCat — purchases and subscriptions

receives: anonymous app user ID + purchase data + device metadata

Coast uses RevenueCat to manage in-app purchases and subscription status, on both stores. RevenueCat receives an anonymous app user ID generated for your install, along with purchase and transaction data (what was bought, when, and whether it is still active), plus the device and app metadata described above. It does not receive your rides, your location, or your identity, and it never sees your card details.

Where purchasing is switched on in the copy of Coast you have installed, RevenueCat is contacted at launch to check your subscription status, whether or not you have ever bought anything. Where it is not switched on, the kit stays dormant and RevenueCat is not contacted at all.

Privacy policy: revenuecat.com/privacy

Payments — Google Play and the App Store

receives: your payment details, never shared with us

Coast never sees or handles your payment details. When you buy Coast Pro, the purchase is processed by the store you installed Coast from: Google Play Billing on Android, or Apple's App Store payment processing on iPhone. Your card and billing details go to Google or to Apple, under their own privacy policies. They are never sent to Coast, and never to RevenueCat.

OneSignal — push notifications

receives: push token + subscription ID + five profile labels + ride and purchase events + device metadata

If — and only if — you accept the notification opt-in, Coast uses OneSignal to deliver push notifications. Until you accept it, OneSignal is held behind a consent gate and collects nothing at all.

Once you accept, OneSignal receives a device push token, an anonymous subscription ID, and five labels used to make messages relevant:

  • your most recent ride score
  • your total number of rides
  • how many of those rides could be scored — a very short or poorly tracked ride cannot be
  • which direction your scores are moving — improving, steady or slipping
  • which vehicle type you selected

Coast also reports a short list of events to OneSignal, so it can tell whether a message actually led to anything rather than only that it was delivered: that you recorded a ride, that you opened the Trends screen, and that you bought Coast Pro. Two of them carry a number as well — the score of the ride you just finished, and the price you paid.

Each label is a single number or word, and each event is a name plus at most one number. Alongside them the kit sends the device and app metadata described at the start of “Services Coast connects to” — device model, operating system version, app and kit version, language, time zone — and, as with any internet request, your IP address. OneSignal never receives your routes, your location, your ride history, or your raw sensor data.

If you decline, or turn Coast's notifications off later in your system settings, none of this applies to you and nothing is sent — Coast checks that permission and withdraws consent when it sees you have turned it off. Coast deliberately has no in-app notification switch: your phone's own notification settings are the single place that controls this. On Android that is Settings → Apps → Coast → Notifications; on iPhone, Settings → Notifications → Coast.

One detail worth being precise about, because it is easy to assume otherwise. On Android you will also see separate channels for Coast's own on-device alerts — the ride score, and the notice that a ride stopped recording — and you can silence one while keeping the other. Those are alerts Coast raises on the phone itself, with no server involved. Muting one of them changes what Coast shows you; it does not withdraw consent, because the consent gate follows Coast's overall notification permission. If your intent is that nothing is sent, turn Coast's notifications off as a whole rather than muting a single channel.

Privacy policy: onesignal.com/privacy_policy

Push delivery — Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple Push Notification service

receives: the notification, in transit

Actually delivering a notification to your phone needs the platform's own transport: Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android, and Apple Push Notification service (APNs) on iPhone. They carry the notification; neither receives your ride data.

CARTO and OpenStreetMap — map tiles

receives: approximate viewing area + IP address

The map you see is made of tile images requested from CARTO's servers, built on OpenStreetMap data. Like any app that shows a map, those requests necessarily reveal the approximate area you are looking at and your IP address to the tile server.

This is a normal consequence of loading map images from the internet, and it is the one place where something location-adjacent leaves your phone. It is the map view you are looking at, not your recorded ride track — your actual GPS log is never sent to them.

Map data is © OpenStreetMap contributors; tiles are © CARTO.

Data retention

Coast keeps your rides on your device until you delete them. There is no expiry and no cleanup on our side, because there is no our side — we do not hold a copy.

Deleting Coast removes the app's storage and every ride in it, including the local database and every CSV file. Two things are worth knowing on iPhone: Offload App deliberately keeps an app's data, so it does not delete your rides; and if your rides were part of an iCloud or computer backup, restoring that backup can bring them back. Deleting rides inside the app removes them from the phone, but it cannot reach into a backup that has already been made — that is managed in your Apple account's backup settings.

Data held by the third parties above (RevenueCat's purchase records, OneSignal's push subscription) is retained according to their own policies, linked in each section.

Your rights and how to exercise them

Because your data is on your device and not on a server, you do not need to ask anyone's permission to control it:

  • Access it — open the app to view your rides, or export the raw CSV files from the developer section in Settings.
  • Delete it — open a ride from History and use Delete this ride to remove it and its raw sensor log. On the free tier History shows your three most recent rides, so use Settings → Delete all rides to erase every ride and log at once, whether or not you can open them individually. Deleting the app also removes everything it holds.
  • Stop collection — do not start a ride, or revoke the location permission in your phone's settings (Android: Settings → Apps → Coast → Permissions; iPhone: Settings → Coast → Location). Coast cannot record without it.
  • Stop notifications — turn Coast's notification permission off in your phone's settings (Android: Settings → Apps → Coast → Notifications; iPhone: Settings → Notifications → Coast).

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights under laws such as the GDPR or CCPA — for example the right to access, correct, or delete personal data held about you. In practice we hold none, so those requests are answered by your own device. For anything relating to purchase records, contact us at the address below and we will help you reach the relevant processor.

Children's privacy

Coast is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has used the app in a way that concerns you, note that any data recorded is on that child's device and can be removed by deleting the rides or uninstalling the app. You are welcome to contact us with questions.

Changes to this policy

If Coast changes what it collects or which services it uses, this policy will be updated and the "Last updated" date at the top will change. Material changes will be noted in the app release notes. Continuing to use Coast after an update means you accept the revised policy.
