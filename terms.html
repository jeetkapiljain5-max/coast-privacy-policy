What Coast is, and what it is not

Coast records a ride you start yourself, using your phone's GPS and motion sensors, and turns it into a score out of 100 based on how many hard brakes and hard launches it picked up and how far you rode. Afterwards it draws your route with those moments pinned where they happened, and estimates how much further the energy you braked away could have carried you. You pick whether you are on a scooter or in a car, because the two brake and pull away very differently.

What the free app does. It records and scores every ride, keeps every ride on your phone, and draws you the full route map for each one, with the rough moments pinned on it. History shows you the three most recent.

What Coast Pro adds. The full history you already have. The trends built from it, once you have more rides than the free three — below that, Trends is not locked, because there would be nothing behind the lock. The coaching line on each ride summary, which says what to change next time; without Pro you get the ride's own finding where there is one to give — how many harsh moments it had and roughly what they cost you in range — and an invitation to unlock the advice. On a ride with no harsh moments, or one too short or too patchy to score, there is no count to give and Coast says that instead. And replay, which scrubs back through your route moment by moment.

Nothing is deleted to make you buy — if you want older rides gone, Settings → Delete all rides erases them whether or not History is showing them to you.

The purchase screen in the app shows the price and the period before you buy. If a later version of Coast changes what Coast Pro includes, the purchase screen in that version is the current list.

What Coast is not:

  • Not a navigation app. It never tells you where to go.
  • Not a speedometer you should rely on. The number comes from GPS.
  • Not a dashcam, a crash detector, or an emergency service.
  • Not a background tracker. It records only while you have a ride running and the app is open in front of you.
  • Not a cloud service. There is no account and no server of ours, so there is nothing to log into and nothing to recover.
  • Not a safety device. That one has its own section, and it is the next one.

Riding safely

If we had to delete everything else on this page, we would keep this.

Coast is a coaching aid, not a safety device

Coast cannot see other vehicles, pedestrians, potholes, weather, or traffic lights. It does not know where the road goes or what the speed limit is. It will never warn you about anything in front of you, because it cannot: everything it tells you is worked out afterwards, from readings your own phone took.

Do not ride as though something is watching the road for you. Nothing is.

Set the phone up before you move, then leave it alone

  • Pick your vehicle and tap start before you set off, while you are stationary. When you arrive, come to a stop first and stop the ride after.
  • Use Pocket Mode. It is the only thing in Coast that holds your screen awake, and it is the way we would rather you rode. It makes the screen look off — black, dimmed, and deaf to every touch except a deliberate press-and-hold — so a pocket cannot press Stop. The screen is still on and the ride keeps recording. Pocket Mode does not truly turn the display off, because a display that really went off would end the ride.
  • If you ride with the phone in a mount, do one of two things first: set your phone's screen timeout to Never, or turn Pocket Mode on anyway. A mount keeps the phone still; it does not keep the screen awake. When your phone's own timeout fires, Coast is pushed into the background and the ride ends there.
  • Coast records only while it is open in front of you. If it leaves the foreground mid-ride — you switch apps, take a call, the screen locks or times out — Coast stops the ride at that point, saves what you rode up to it, and, if you have Coast's notifications turned on, posts a notification saying so. It does not resume by itself.

The notification that says your ride stopped

This one depends on notifications being on. If you said no to notifications, or turned them off later, nothing tells you mid-ride that recording has stopped — you find that out when you next pick the phone up, and the rest of this section does not apply to you. Turning Coast's notifications on is what makes it arrive.

That notification fires the moment recording stops, not when you get back to the phone. It can arrive while you are still moving, and on Android it is set to come through as a heads-up alert rather than wait quietly, because it is the one message a rider genuinely needs.

Ignore it until you have stopped. There is nothing to do about it in the moment. The part of the ride you already rode is already saved, and no amount of looking at the phone brings the rest of the ride back. Reaching for a mounted phone at speed to read a notification is the exact risk this section exists to remove.

If you see that notification often, that is your screen timeout ending your rides. Fix it once, with Pocket Mode or a longer timeout, and it stops happening.

The live rough-moment count is not for reading in traffic

The ride screen shows a running count of rough moments. It is there for when you are stopped — at a light, at the end of the ride — not for glancing at while you ride. There is nothing it can tell you mid-journey that is worth a look away from the road.

If you catch yourself looking at it while moving, that is the moment to switch on Pocket Mode.

Never hold the phone in your hand while riding. On a two-wheeler that is one hand off the bars, and nothing on a screen is worth that.

If you use a mount, check that it holds before you go. A phone that works loose at speed is a hazard to you and to everyone behind you.

The road law is yours to follow, and Coast does not know it

You are responsible for riding legally. Coast has no knowledge of the rules where you are: it never displays a speed limit, never tells you one, and has no concept of licences, helmets, insurance, or who is allowed to ride what.

If the law where you ride says a phone may not be mounted, or may not be visible, or may not be running while you drive, then that law wins. Do not run Coast.

A score is never a reason to ride differently in traffic

Coast judges braking and acceleration against physics, not against the situation you were in. It cannot tell an emergency stop from an impatient one.

So if you have to brake hard, brake hard. Swerve. Do whatever the road needs. Coast will record it as a rough moment and your score will drop, and that is the correct outcome — take the points and stay upright. A ride you had to save is worth more than a hundred.

Do not ride to raise a score. Do not roll gently towards a hazard to protect an average. Do not treat anyone's score as something to beat on a public road.

Smoother is not the same as safer

Smooth riding is usually safer riding, which is most of the point of Coast. But smoothness is the only thing Coast measures. It knows nothing about your speed, your following distance, your lane position, or whether you were looking where you were going. A high score means you rode gently. It does not mean you rode well, and it is not permission to relax.

Scores are estimates, not measurements

Every number Coast shows you is worked out from a phone strapped to a moving vehicle. Treat it as a good indication, not as an instrument reading.

  • Speed and distance come from GPS, which drifts under bridges, between buildings, and in bad weather. Coast is not a calibrated speedometer, and you should never use its speed to decide whether you are within a limit.
  • Hard braking and hard launching are inferred from accelerometer and GPS readings crossing thresholds we chose. Where your phone sits, how rigid the mount is, the road surface, and the phone model itself all change what gets detected. Two phones on the same journey can score differently.
  • The energy or fuel you wasted is a model's estimate, not a reading from your vehicle. Nothing in Coast is connected to the vehicle at all.
  • Some rides cannot be scored, and Coast says so rather than guessing. A ride shorter than about 300 metres or a minute, or one where the tracking was too patchy to trust, shows a dash instead of a number. Trends need five scored rides before Coast will call a direction.

None of this is a measurement of you as a rider, and none of it is evidence. Do not use it as either.
